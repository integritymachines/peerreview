Domain and target estimand
The question concerns drug treatment and survival among men living with HIV who initially have neither an AIDS-defining illness nor antiretroviral treatment. The target is the difference in subsequent mortality under specified histories of starting zidovudine compared with remaining untreated, for the eligible population. The contrast must specify when treatment starts and what continued treatment means; a single undated treated-versus-untreated label is insufficient.

Observed variables and evidence
Repeated participant records include age, calendar time, zidovudine initiation, CD4 and CD8 lymphocyte counts, white and red blood cell counts, platelets, clinical symptoms, AIDS-defining illness, death, and continued participation. Participants return periodically for questionnaires, physical examination, and blood collection. Recruitment occurs in several metropolitan areas, and eligibility begins during the period in which the drug becomes available. Follow-up ends at death, the administrative end of observation, or an extended interval after the last visit. The source reports actual participant observations rather than an invented treatment example. Individual records are not supplied within the article; the available evidence comprises descriptions of collection, tabulations, and documentation of the recorded histories.

Dependencies requiring scrutiny
Treatment use, immune status, symptoms, disease progression, and survival can be statistically dependent. The same variables recur at successive visits, and dependence may link previous treatment to later health and previous health to later treatment. These are possibilities to be distinguished rather than fixed orientations inferred from co-occurrence alone. Continuing participation may also be related to health and treatment access. Calendar time can accompany changes in care, eligibility, and drug availability. Unrecorded disease severity or clinical information may connect several measured quantities.

Negotiable domain and institutional priors
A clinician may use immune markers and symptoms when deciding whether to start therapy. A proposed biological account allows the drug to alter viral replication and thereby later immune function, but the magnitude and completeness of that account are empirical questions. Treating initiation as permanent continued use, ignoring differences in dose and adherence, and summarizing treatment history by current status are simplifications, not physical requirements. Absence of additional common determinants, comparability of people with the same recorded histories, and completeness of follow-up explanations also require substantive support.

Physical constraints and observation limits
An ingested drug cannot act before administration; a blood specimen assay records properties of the collected specimen rather than assigning the participant's prior exposure. Death terminates that person's subsequent living physiology and treatment consumption. These constraints do not establish every arrow among visits or biomarkers. Periodic measurements miss changes between visits, and initiation records need not establish actual ingestion. Restriction to selected surviving, untreated men limits population coverage. Measured blood counts are incomplete descriptions of disease state, while loss of participation can remove outcome or treatment information.
