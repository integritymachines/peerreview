Domain and target estimand
The question concerns intracellular signalling in primary human immune cells. The target is the pattern of influence among measured protein and lipid activities under specified molecular perturbations, including whether changing one component would alter another in this cellular setting. An influence can be mediated through unmeasured molecules; a measured relationship need not represent direct molecular binding.

Observed variables and evidence
Individual cells are exposed to receptor stimulation and to additional reagents intended to stimulate or inhibit particular signalling processes. Conditions include receptor-directed antibodies, an adhesion-related stimulus, small-molecule inhibitors, a phorbol ester, and a cyclic nucleotide analogue. Reactions are stopped by fixation after a prescribed stimulation interval. Fluorescent probes and multiparameter flow cytometry record several activities simultaneously in each fixed cell. Recorded entities include Raf, Mek, Erk, Akt, Jnk, p38, phospholipase C gamma, protein kinase C, a protein kinase A substrate signal, and phosphoinositide lipids. The measured quantities concern particular phosphorylation sites, substrate recognition, or lipid abundance rather than an unrestricted measure of each protein's entire activity. Cell-level records from multiple reagent conditions and documentation of reagent targets are available as evidence; repeated trajectories of the same living cell are not.

Dependencies requiring scrutiny
Joint fluctuations may occur among these signalling quantities within a condition, and their joint distributions may differ across reagent conditions. Potential connections can include cross-talk, multiple routes between components, feedback, and shared influences from components outside the measurement panel. Receptor activation, cell state, and reagent exposure may be associated with many recorded signals. Such dependencies do not by themselves settle molecular direction, directness, or the absence of a hidden intermediary.

Negotiable biological and functional priors
Prior biochemical knowledge proposes activities and targets for the applied reagents and possible relations among the measured components. This knowledge can constrain interpretation only to the extent that target specificity, cell-type relevance, and dose-dependent behaviour hold here. A reagent intended for one target can act elsewhere or perturb several processes. Assuming that a phosphorylation measurement is a faithful proxy for the relevant functional activity is also negotiable. Treating fixed-cell observations as representative of a stable cellular condition, excluding feedback, or assuming that all relevant common influences were measured are scientific restrictions, not conservation laws. Variation across cells can reflect both signalling variation and heterogeneous starting states.

Physical constraints and observation limits
Adding a reagent is a physical manipulation, and fixation terminates the sampled living response; the ensuing assay cannot retrospectively alter that response. Chemical modification requires an actual molecular process, but this fact alone does not orient the measured network. A fixed snapshot cannot reveal the order of changes within an individual cell. Probe specificity, fluorescence measurement, unmeasured molecular species, and selection of the sampled cell population limit interpretation. Information about molecular location, other phosphorylation sites, and intervening events is incomplete.
