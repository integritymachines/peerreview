Domain and target estimand
The setting is school-based parasite control, child health, and education in rural western Kenya. The target is the effect of offering a school deworming package on pupil health and school participation, including effects on pupils who do not themselves receive medicine and on pupils at nearby schools. Academic performance is a separate outcome. The relevant intervention includes medicine and prevention education, so a medicine-only effect is a distinct target.

Observed variables and evidence availability
Seventy-five primary schools serving more than 30,000 pupils entered a staged program. Schools were grouped using administrative zone and participation in other assistance programs, ordered alphabetically, and every third school was assigned to one of three groups of twenty-five. A stochastic starting step is not specified in the supplied procedural description. One group began in 1998, another in 1999, and the remaining group began in 2001. The first two years provide the focal observations. Medicines were provided under prevalence-based school protocols. The protocol excluded girls aged thirteen and older because of pregnancy-related concerns. Consent procedures changed from community consent with parental opt-out to signed parental consent. Actual receipt, eligibility, and program availability are therefore distinct variables. Pupil and school questionnaires, treatment records, school locations and enrollments, unannounced attendance observations, tests, and selected parasitological and health measurements are available. Health sampling differs across groups and years.

Undirected dependencies
Candidate dependencies include parasite burden–health, health–attendance, attendance–learning, sanitation–infection exposure, water contact–infection exposure, household resources–participation, and geography–environmental exposure. School proximity and population density can link exposure across pupils and schools. Consent, presence on medicine-delivery days, and family attitudes may connect actual receipt with later outcomes. These are candidate relationships without an asserted empirical magnitude or fixed orientation for all variables.

Negotiable institutional and functional prior knowledge
School allocation, the staged schedule, eligibility, medicine-delivery rules, and consent requirements are documented features. Environmental transmission provides a reason to consider connections beyond a recipient. The exact radius, magnitude, species mix, and direction of indirect health or schooling responses are not guaranteed. Equating school distance with personal exposure, assuming no effects beyond the observed area, and treating consent or attendance as unrelated to health are unverified assumptions. Health education and medicine may act through different channels. Infection can recur, and repeated exposure need not have a constant response.

Physically constrained directional knowledge
Administration of a drug requires the child to receive a dose. Infection requires exposure to viable parasites through the relevant biological route. These constraints do not establish that treatment of a particular nearby child changes another child's exposure or that a measured infection change causes an attendance change. The supplied setting does not warrant fixing those orientations from speculative biological stories or from measurement dates alone.

Observation limitations
Not every child has every health measurement, and baseline parasitology is not available symmetrically across all groups. Birth-month information is often missing, complicating precise eligibility classification. Enrollment, transfer, absence, consent, and actual drug receipt are separate processes. Attendance observations and test participation do not capture every day or every learning input. Household contacts, travel, and environmental contamination are incompletely measured.
