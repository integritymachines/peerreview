Domain and target estimand
The setting is temporary employment assistance for disadvantaged adults in the United States during the mid-1970s. The target is the effect of access to supported work, including a temporary paid job, supervised work experience, and counseling, on earnings after the program. Separate populations include women receiving public assistance and disadvantaged men; these populations need not have the same response. Earnings during a subsidized placement and earnings after leaving it are different outcomes.

Observed variables and evidence availability
Qualified applicants at ten sites were allocated randomly between access to the program and no program access. Offered placements generally lasted nine to eighteen months, depending on site and target group. Crews contained three to five participants. Work tasks varied across sites and participant groups. Baseline and subsequent interviews contain demographic information and earnings, with follow-up scheduled at nine-month intervals. Available characteristics include age, education, race, marital status, and prior earnings. Public assistance receipt and household composition are available for relevant comparison populations. Additional records come from a household panel and a population survey linked to social-security earnings; candidate comparison populations can be restricted using employment, age, household, and assistance characteristics. The records contain both earlier earnings and later earnings, rather than just a single contemporaneous outcome.

Undirected dependencies
The ex-ante dependency inventory includes education–earnings, age–earnings, prior–subsequent earnings, household responsibilities–employment, assistance receipt–employment, local labor conditions–earnings, and site–work content. Motivation, health, and job opportunities may be related to both program seeking and later employment. These are candidate relationships, not empirical claims or fixed orientations. Shared crew and local-market conditions could connect participants' outcomes.

Negotiable institutional and functional prior knowledge
Eligibility rules, the allocation procedure, placement duration, wage schedules, and the scheduled interviews are documented institutional facts. Transferability between applicants and people sampled from the general population remains an assumption. Stability of an earnings process, adequate measurement of work readiness, and equivalence of earnings definitions across sources are contestable. A paid placement may provide experience, contacts, income, or time away from other job search; neither the dominant channel nor a constant response is institutionally guaranteed.

Physically constrained directional knowledge
Placement-pay rules and earnings accounting are institutional or definitional constraints, not physically necessary directions. No domain-specific physical constraint establishing the target employment or earnings response is supplied. Calendar order alone does not establish the direction of associations among education, selection, and employment.

Observation limitations
Interview follow-up is incomplete and differs by interview horizon and participant population. Earnings definitions and observation windows differ across sources. Self-reports, administrative coverage, departures from a placement, and unrecorded alternatives to program work may limit comparability. General-population records do not reveal every applicant characteristic or every local opportunity. Complete individual employment histories and all mechanisms affecting later earnings are unavailable.
