Framework vocabulary (not mutually exclusive schools):
- potential_outcomes: Rubin/Neyman treatment- or regime-indexed counterfactual outcomes and assignment/identification restrictions. This does not inherently require randomization or no unmeasured confounding.
- structural_causal_model: Pearl-style causal assignments with exogenous variables and intervention/counterfactual semantics, possibly graphical. A generic regression or Bayesian network is insufficient evidence. Independence, acyclicity, faithfulness and modularity must be stated where used, not silently presumed.
- structural_econometrics: Heckman/Haavelmo-style behavioral, selection, choice, equilibrium or policy-invariance models supplying counterfactual mechanisms. Optimization and equilibrium are assumptions, not physical laws. This framework can use potential outcomes and need not be a parametric Heckman correction.
- mechanistic_dynamical: an explicit mechanistic state-evolution/intervention model grounded in relevant physical or biological processes; distinguish empirically supported constraints from assumed compartment equations and numerical calibration. Can overlap structural causal and econometric representations.
- other: name and describe any additional causal framework; do not use as a bin for an estimator.
A hybrid is an explicitly compatible combination of two or more of these components, represented in 'hybrids', not an exclusive sixth school. Several frameworks can express the same estimand and assumptions. Mathematical representability, point identification, practical convenience and policy extrapolation are distinct claims.
