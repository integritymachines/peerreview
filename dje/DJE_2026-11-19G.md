### 1. Bach, Bozio, Guillouzouic, Leroy, Malgouyres — A Closely-Held View of Dividend Taxation (CEPR DP 22173, Nov 2026, posted 13 Nov; author PDF dated Nov 2026; an earlier version circulated as "Follow the Money! Why Dividends Overreact to Flat-Tax Reforms")
Links: https://ideas.repec.org/p/cpr/ceprdp/22173.html · PDF: https://www.parisschoolofeconomics.com/bozio-antoine/fr/documents/BBGLM_dividends.pdf · https://cepr.org/publications/dp22173 (cepr.org returns 403 to this fetcher)
**Question:** Why do dividends respond strongly to dividend taxes while investment does not? Which theory of dividend taxation fits closely-held firms?
**Method:**
- Data: a new French shareholder registry linking all personal and corporate income tax returns (2006–2021), plus wealth-tax records.
- Design: DiD around two reforms in opposite directions. In 2013 the top dividend rate rose from 36.5% to 40.2%; in 2018 it fell from 40.2% to 30%.
- Household level: households in the top 1% of wealth, with treatment assigned by pre-reform "potential income" (income plus retained earnings in controlled firms).
- Firm level: treated firms are ≥50% controlled by large individual shareholders, at least one of them liable for wealth tax. Control firms have <10% such ownership.
- Model: a "closely-held view" nesting the old and new views. Owners have a costly, lower-taxed alternative payout (saving inside the firm) and are averse to dilution.
**Result:**
- Dividend tax elasticities are 4.3 (2013 hike) and 2.2 (2018 cut) at firm level, and 3.8/2.1 at household level.
- In 2018, firm owners had an elasticity of 2.5 versus 0.4 for non-owners.
- Investment shows no response. Per euro of pre-reform assets: −0.0003 (SE 0.0015) in 2013 and −0.0001 (SE 0.0017) in 2018, against a treated pre-reform mean of 0.040. The CI excludes roughly ±7% of the mean.
- Retained earnings mirror dividends: 2013 +0.0055 vs −0.0109; 2018 −0.0092 vs +0.0083.
- At face value France was on the wrong side of the dividend Laffer curve: the hike lost €3 per €1 of mechanical revenue, and the cut gained €1.7. Counting deferred capital-gains taxation attenuates this or reverses it.
**Beyond prior work:**
- Yagan (2015), Alstadsæter et al. (2017) and Bilicka et al. (2025) found large payout responses and no investment response. This paper explains why and decomposes the dividend response inside the firm.
- It uses two opposite-signed reforms and household-level first stages.
- It supersedes the investment claim of Boissel–Matray (AER 2022, retracted 2023). The same team's AER comment (Bach et al. 2023a) is cited in a footnote.
**Surviving claim:** In France, dividend taxes on owner-managed firms shift income between dividends and corporate saving, with no detectable effect on investment. Measured elasticities and top-income shares therefore overstate both the distortion and the redistribution.
**Decisive limitations:**
- The control group is small and unusual: about 11k firms against 82k (2013) and 150k (2018) treated firms. It consists of firms with no large individual owner and no group integration, so comparability rests on the fixed effects and pre-trends.
- Concurrent wealth-tax changes may confound both reforms; see #S. This check found no text discussion of them.
- Revenue and Laffer conclusions depend on assumed later taxation of retained earnings.
**Why selected:** It is the most consequential public-finance contribution in the window. It uses two opposite-signed reforms on universal linked tax data and reaches a direct design conclusion for capital-income taxation.

### 2. Gunsilius, Van Dijcke, Wüthrich — Inference After Coding Agents (arXiv 2611.23174v1 [stat.ME, cross-listed econ.EM], 17 Nov 2026; replication package on GitHub)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23174
**Question:** Do AI coding agents that run empirical analyses invalidate classical p-values through selection? Can this be detected and corrected without modelling the agent?
**Method:**
- Formalizes agentic selective inference.
- Proposes a replay correction: re-run the agent with fresh randomness on subsamples (m/n ≈ 0.1), sorted into "favourable" (tail) and "body" strata by the selected analysis's p-value. Then estimate the selection multiplier λ, the relative probability of reselecting the same analysis when it looks significant, and correct the p-value (building on Sood 2024).
- Proves asymptotic validity conditional on selection under i.i.d. subsampling, recurrence and selection stability. Uniform validity needs a stronger uniform-recurrence assumption.
- Simulations and two applications: Card–Krueger 1994, and immigration/welfare data from Brady–Finnigan 2014 / Breznau et al. 2022.
**Result:**
- On null data, four frontier agents asked for "one compelling, defensible finding" report p ≤ 0.05 in 54%–98% of runs (97 of 99 in the open-ended design).
- Corrected false-rejection rates are 4.2% (fixed-rule), 5.1% (open-ended agents) and 5.9% (placebo real data, 102 datasets from three studies).
- Median estimated λ is 19. Agents' own multiplicity corrections do not restore validity.
**Beyond prior work:** Earlier selective-inference methods (Fithian et al.; Berk et al.; black-box selective inference, Liu–Panigrahi) need a model of selection or restrict the selector. This method exploits the fact that agents can be replayed, and it learns the selection from the agent's behaviour. It goes beyond agent-variation and specification-search studies (Breznau et al. 2022; Miao et al. 2026) by restoring guarantees rather than only documenting dispersion.
**Surviving claim:** Agent-driven analyses can massively inflate false positives even under mild prompts. When the agent's selection is stable and recurrent, a black-box replay correction restores approximately nominal size.
**Decisive limitations:**
- Costly: 640 replays per analysis in the open-ended design. Corrected-p confidence bands are wide (mean 0.71; 0.44 excluding tail-only cases), so power and informativeness are limited.
- Requires i.i.d.-type subsampling units and recurrence. The authors note size distortions can persist along sequences of nulls without uniform recurrence.
- Does not address human selection over prompts or runs.
- The inflation rates are specific to particular model versions and prompts.
**Why selected:** It is timely and consequential for the credibility of empirical economics. It is a rigorous, implementable proposal with a replication package.

### 3. Grigolon, Meeker, von Ditfurth — Does Peter Piper Pick Pepper Inattentively? Consumer Inattention to Package Content (CEPR DP 22181, Nov 2026; author PDF dated 15 Sep 2026; extends the Meeker–von Ditfurth paper posted on SSRN in 2024)
Links: https://ideas.repec.org/p/cpr/ceprdp/22181.html · PDF: https://lauragrig.github.io/website/20260915_GrigolonMeekerVonDitfurth.pdf
**Question:** Do consumers notice "shrinkflation" (package downsizing)? What are the welfare effects of disclosure versus a ban?
**Method:**
- NielsenIQ retail scanner data for pepper (2014–2016), with 30 downsizing episodes (15 branded, 15 private-label) covering about a third of category sales. The main episode is McCormick's 2014 fill reduction, which led to litigation.
- Random-coefficient logit (BLP-type) with a Bernoulli attention coefficient. Attention is identified from (a) retailers stocking old and new UPCs side by side and (b) share responses deviating from pre-period weight preferences.
- Price instruments are cost shifters (wholesale pepper) and Gandhi–Houde instruments.
- Supply-side counterfactuals.
**Result:**
- The attention probability is at the boundary. The profiled GMM test rejects any value above 0.00056 (about 1 consumer in 1,800).
- Price sensitivity far exceeds sensitivity to net weight.
- Disclosure: downsized products' share falls from 31.2% to 24.1%, and consumer surplus rises 0.78% of pepper spending (≈$613k/yr in sampled retailers). A back-of-envelope extrapolation to all groceries gives ≈$0.5bn/yr.
- Ban: prices rise $0.36 (+9.6%) and consumer surplus rises only 0.43%, because an extra ounce costs ≈$0.48 but is valued at ≈$0.36.
**Beyond prior work:** Previous downsizing studies (Cakir–Balagtas 2014; Yonezawa–Richards 2016) do not identify inattention. This paper identifies inattention to quantity within a standard demand system and ranks disclosure above a ban, which is directly relevant to the 2024 French and 2026 Austrian rules.
**Surviving claim:** In this category, virtually no shoppers noticed downsizing at the point of sale. Disclosure beats a ban on consumer surplus, but the ranking depends on how marginal cost scales with fill.
**Decisive limitations:**
- One small category, so external validity is open.
- The boundary estimate with aggregated retailer-DMA-month data probably overstates precision; the "1 in 1,800" bound is model-conditional.
- The ban result depends on the cost-curvature specification, as the authors note.
- Learning through repeated purchases is not identified (median 22 weeks between purchases).
**Why selected:** It is a credible structural measurement of a salient consumer-protection question, and it reaches a non-obvious policy ranking.

### 4. Nocke, Sato — Oligopoly with Mixed Bundling (CEPR DP 22164, posted 9 Nov 2026; draft dated 24 Oct 2026; delayed-indexed, first seen this run)
Links: https://ideas.repec.org/p/cpr/ceprdp/22164.html · PDF: https://volkernocke.com/NS-OMiBun.pdf
**Question:** How do multiproduct oligopolists set prices and bundle discounts? What do entry, cost changes and conglomerate mergers do to consumer surplus?
**Method:**
- Two markets, with multinomial-logit demand over product pairs and outside options (markets need not be covered).
- Arbitrary numbers of firms, portfolios, valuations and costs.
- Reduces each firm's problem to a common absolute markup through a relaxed problem (extending Nocke–Schutz 2018) and proves a strictly concave ordinal potential.
**Result:**
- Lemma 1: each firm sets the same absolute markup on every product and bundle, so a bundle's second item is effectively sold at cost.
- Theorem 1: the equilibrium exists and is unique.
- Strategic complementarity is signed by the "lift" between firms.
- A "type network" (standalone, cross-firm and outside types) summarises primitives.
- A firm-wide uniform cost cut and entry both raise consumer surplus, although entry can raise some incumbents' markups.
- A three-step merger decomposition with lift cutoffs. The welfare effects of mixed bundling depend on market coverage and asymmetries.
**Beyond prior work:** Earlier mixed-bundling oligopoly results (Matutes–Regibeau 1992; Armstrong–Vickers 2010; Zhou 2021) are limited to duopoly, symmetric or covered markets. This paper gives the first general existence and uniqueness result usable for applied merger analysis with asymmetric portfolios.
**Surviving claim:** Under logit demand over pairs, mixed-bundling oligopoly is tractable and has a unique equilibrium. Merger and entry comparative statics are signed by low-dimensional types.
**Decisive limitations:**
- The results lean on MNL/IIA over bundles, two markets and one unit per market.
- The common-markup result, and hence "second item at cost", is a logit property. Robustness to nested or mixed logit is unknown.
- No empirical calibration.
**Why selected:** It is the strongest theory contribution in the window and immediately usable for conglomerate-merger policy, including telecoms, insurance, and energy plus broadband.

### WJE selections
**1. Finkelstein, Notowidigdo, Rafkin — When Food Stamps Became Free (NBER WP 35996, v1) https://www.nber.org/papers/w35996**
- *Deeper check, the 1979–82 confound:*
  - The authors model local business cycles explicitly with a proxy from industries with few food-stamp workers, using the Freyaldenhoven et al. (2019) proxy approach. This removes slight enrollment pre-trends and leaves the magnitudes unchanged (§ "Identification and Robustness").
  - Data are extended to 1984 in robustness (fn. 14). There are also state×year fixed effects, a top-two-quartile sample, and WIC/CHC rollout controls.
- *Verified limitation:* the identifying variation comes almost entirely from 1970 poverty rates. Poverty-decile×year controls eliminate the effect (fn. 23). Identification therefore rests on no other poverty-correlated shock in 1979, and the business-cycle proxy is the only direct defence.
- *Correction to earlier issues:* on rereading, the authors' central normative ranking is W(0.65) > W(1) > 1. Under their redistributive weights, the free-stamp program still raises social welfare per dollar relative to financing nothing (W(1) > 1 with a nondistortionary tax on the average person). It does so by less than the earlier purchase-requirement regime. Compliers' welfare-weighted valuation is 1.1, against 2.2 for always-takers. The authors report this ranking robust to relaxed assumptions (App. C.5). The 13 Nov issue's statement that free stamps "raised social welfare" should be read as W(1) > 1. It does not mean free stamps beat the purchase-requirement regime: the authors find the opposite.
- *Assessment:* the enrollment response (+1.85 pp) and the precise birth-weight null survive. The welfare conclusions are model- and weight-dependent, as the authors stress. The finding transports only partly to EBT-era SNAP.
- *Why WJE:* it combines credibility, policy stakes ($100bn/yr program) and a conceptual payoff on ordeals and targeting better than any other pool paper.

**2. Gagliardone, Gertler, Lenzu, Tielens, Ebsim — An Odd Approximation of Inflation for State Dependent Pricing Models (NBER WP 36016, v1) https://www.nber.org/papers/w36016**
- *Deeper check, sectoral and energy shocks:*
  - The cost-push shock is a reduced-form marginal-cost wedge that "stands in for supply-side disturbances, such as oil or energy price increases" (§2, after Clarida et al. 1999).
  - The authors explicitly leave multi-sector economies and the pass-through of large energy, tariff or devaluation shocks to future work (pp. 40–41).
  - Cost-push and demand shifts both enter firms' nominal marginal cost. The state-dependent block therefore amplifies both alike, so the demand/cost-push split is pinned down by output, policy-rate and frequency co-movement in a three-shock, one-sector model. The 48%→7% (US) reduction in the cost-push share is best read as model-conditional.
- *Assessment:* the methodological contribution survives intact: a closed-form, estimable state-dependent pricing block with selection. The historical reinterpretation is a strong but provisional hypothesis.
- *Why WJE over w36004:* the tool is durable and portable to richer models, and its central approximation is verified against global solutions. w36004's monetary-versus-information interpretation of the non-yield shock is unresolved.

**3. Gunsilius, Van Dijcke, Wüthrich — Inference After Coding Agents (arXiv 2611.23174v1) https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23174**
- *Deeper check:*
  - Simulation designs (Table 1): fixed-rule selector with 400 datasets (n = 1,000, m = 31); open-ended agents with 99 datasets (n = 2,000, m = 200, 640 replays); real-data placebo with 102 datasets.
  - Corrected size is 4.2%, 5.1% and 5.9%. The 5.9% pooled real-data figure is slightly above nominal.
  - The authors themselves note that, without uniform recurrence, size distortions can persist along sequences of nulls, because n^−1/2 signals that drive selection at n vanish at m.
  - Replay agents noticed codebook/sample mismatches and had to be fed consistent "genuine" subsample metadata. Implementations must reproduce this.
- *Assessment:* the theory is sound for the stated conditions, and the empirical demonstration is striking. The correction is expensive and imprecise (wide p-value bands), so in practice it is better at detecting agentic selection than at producing sharp corrected inference.
- *Why WJE:* it has the broadest consequence for empirical economics' credibility this week. It does not overlap the other picks.

## #S — Scorecards
Severity: fatal/critical/fixable. Visibility: obvious/tricky/hidden. Color shows repair effort: 🟢 none, 🟡 local, 🟠 substantial, 🔴 fundamental.

### CEPR 22173 Bach et al.
**Traditions:**
- Corporate-finance user-cost theory: Auerbach; Poterba–Summers; Chetty–Saez agency view.
- Quasi-experimental public finance: Yagan 2015; Saez–Slemrod–Giertz elasticity of taxable income.
- Income-shifting/avoidance: Gordon–Slemrod; Alstadsæter–Jacob.

**Summary:** Two opposite-signed French reforms produce dividend elasticities of 2–4, driven by closely-held firms. Investment is unchanged and retained earnings absorb the response. A "closely-held view" model rationalizes this and halves the apparent revenue and inequality effects.

**Flags:**
- **Alternatives (GE5/LP6): 🟠 critical-for-2018 / hidden / unresolved suspicion.**
  - France's wealth tax had an income-based cap (plafonnement). By this reviewer's institutional knowledge, not verified here, the cap was reinstated in 2013, and the ISF was replaced by the real-estate-only IFI in 2018.
  - Both changes alter top wealth-holders' incentive to keep income inside firms in the same direction as the dividend-tax changes. The sample is wealth-taxpayers, so the confound loads on the treated group.
  - A text search found no discussion of the cap or of the IFI beyond data labels.
  - Defense: business assets were largely exempt from the ISF, the opposite-signed design, and household-level MTR variation.
  - Repair: substantial (cap-binding indicators, or excluding cap-eligible households).
- **Warrant / comparability (ME3): 🟡 fixable / tricky / verified.**
  - The firm-level control is about 11k firms against 82k–150k treated (Table 4) and is defined by having no large individual owner.
  - Pre-trends are flat (Fig. 3–4), and there are rich industry/closure/age/legal-form/region × year fixed effects.
  - Repair: local (reweighting or matching on size and leverage).
- **Calibration (LP8): 🟡 / obvious / addressed.** The Laffer reversal assumes no later taxation of retained earnings, and the authors show that taxing them attenuates or reverses it.
- **Precedence: 🟢.** It cites the retracted Boissel–Matray result only through the authors' own AER comment (footnote). A main-text contrast would help readers.

**Strongest surviving claim:** investment is insensitive to dividend taxes for French closely-held firms (tight nulls), while payouts are highly elastic.

### arXiv 2611.23174 Gunsilius, Van Dijcke, Wüthrich
**Traditions:**
- Selective/post-selection inference: Berk et al. 2013; Fithian–Sun–Taylor; Andrews–Kitagawa–McCloskey.
- Decision-theoretic econometrics of specification search: Leamer; White 2000; McCloskey–Michaillat.
- Design-based metascience: Breznau et al. 2022; Huntington-Klein et al. 2021.

**Summary:** Coding agents searching over analyses inflate null rejection rates to 54–98%. Replaying the agent on subsamples estimates a selection multiplier λ that restores nominal size.

**Flags:**
- **Warrant (ME5): 🟡 critical-for-practice / tricky / verified (authors' own discussion).**
  - Validity is pointwise without uniform recurrence. Selection driven by n^−1/2 signals can leave persistent distortions.
  - Subsample metadata must be faked consistently (agents noticed mismatches).
  - Defense: Assumption 7 gives uniform results.
  - Repair: local.
- **Calibration / decision usefulness: 🟡 / obvious / verified.** With 640 replays, corrected-p 95% bands average 0.44–0.71 wide, so the method detects selection better than it delivers sharp inference.
- **Scope: 🟢 / obvious.** It does not cover human prompt-shopping or other non-replayable selection. Agent-specific inflation rates are perishable, as the authors note.
- **Integrity: 🟢.** AI-assistance disclosure is included and a replication package is public.

**Strongest surviving claim:** the black-box replay correction is valid under stated stability conditions and is empirically near-nominal (4.2–5.9%).

### CEPR 22181 Grigolon, Meeker, von Ditfurth
**Traditions:**
- Structural IO demand: Berry–Levinsohn–Pakes; Gandhi–Houde.
- Behavioral IO and limited attention: DellaVigna; Chetty–Looney–Kroft.
- Consumer-protection law and economics: Shavell; disclosure regulation.

**Summary:** Random-coefficient logit with a Bernoulli attention coefficient finds near-zero attention to pepper downsizing. Disclosure raises consumer surplus about twice as much as a ban.

**Flags:**
- **Warrant / inference (ME5): 🟡 fixable / tricky / suspicion.**
  - The attention estimate lies on the boundary, and the profiled χ² bound (≤0.00056) uses aggregated shares, whose effective sample is far smaller than the purchase count.
  - Defense: identical results on overlap-only and non-overlap samples, a Monte Carlo recovers η, and pre-period taste stability holds.
  - Repair: local (cluster-robust profile or bootstrap).
- **Alternatives (MI3): 🟡 / tricky / addressed.**
  - A store-level choice-set mismatch would mimic inattention. The overlap/non-overlap robustness addresses this.
  - Learning is not identified.
- **Calibration (MI10): 🟡 / obvious / acknowledged.** The ban-vs-disclosure ranking depends on cost curvature. The $0.5bn extrapolation is back-of-envelope.

**Strongest surviving claim:** at point of sale, almost no pepper buyers noticed downsizing.

### CEPR 22164 Nocke, Sato
**Traditions:**
- Multiproduct oligopoly and aggregative games: Nocke–Schutz; Anderson–de Palma–Thisse.
- Bundling theory: Adams–Yellen; McAfee–McMillan–Whinston; Armstrong–Vickers.
- Antitrust merger analysis: Farrell–Shapiro; Whinston.

**Summary:** Under MNL demand over product pairs, each firm sets a common absolute markup. The reduced game has a strictly concave potential and so a unique equilibrium, and comparative statics are signed by "lift" and a type network.

**Flags:**
- **Calibration / scope (MI5): 🟠 substantial / obvious / verified.**
  - The IIA over bundles drives the common-markup and "second item at cost" result, as in Nocke–Schutz (2018).
  - Results are restricted to two markets with unit demand.
  - Defense: MNL is standard in merger simulation.
  - Repair: substantial (nested or mixed logit).
- **Formal (MI8/MI9): 🟢 / tricky / checked at sketch level.**
  - The relaxed-problem argument and the log-sum-exp concavity of the potential were checked at sketch level.
  - Appendix proofs were not checked line by line.
  - The original (unreduced) game is not a potential game (App. A.1.2), but all equilibria are outcome-equivalent.

**Strongest surviving claim:** existence and uniqueness for general asymmetric mixed-bundling oligopoly under MNL, with tractable merger and entry analysis.
