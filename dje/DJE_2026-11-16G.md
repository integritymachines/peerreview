### 1. Gennaioli, List, Rodemeier, Sun — Irregular Demand: The Anti-Compromise Effect in the Field (NBER WP 36013, released 16 Nov 2026, v1) https://www.nber.org/papers/w36013
**Question:** Does adding a premium product ever raise demand for an existing option? Neoclassical "regularity" says it cannot.
**Method:** Natural field experiment at Lyft (5 Nov 2020 – 20 Jan 2021). 7,242,942 riders were randomized at the rider level, across 47.6m sessions. Treated riders also saw a premium "Fast Pass" option alongside Wait & Save and Standard, at one of three randomized markups (High ≈ 4× Low). Fast Pass was available in ≈33% of treated sessions. Analysis uses ITT regressions with rider-clustered SEs. There is a memory/salience model of attention to prices.
**Result:** First session: Standard falls 2.4/1.8/1.4 pp and Fast Pass takes 2.5/1.5/1.0 pp (Low/Med/High). Wait & Save **rises** 0.2 pp (Med) and 0.3 pp (High), against a 10.7% base. Not buying rises ~0.1–0.2 pp (base 40.6%). The effects grow with price and with repeated exposure: over all sessions the Wait & Save effect is 4–5× larger. They persist in sessions where Fast Pass is not shown. The authors map the effect to a 1.25% Wait & Save price cut and a ~0.9–1.2% across-the-board price rise.
**Beyond prior work:** Previous field tests found a compromise effect (Gu et al. 2018) or no effect (Rafai et al. 2022). This is a large-scale, randomized, repeated-exposure violation of regularity in the opposite ("anti-compromise") direction. It sits uneasily with menu-inference and rational-inattention accounts.
**Surviving claim:** On a real platform with experienced users, a randomly priced premium tier raised price sensitivity and pushed riders down the menu and out of the market. The effect grew with exposure.
**Decisive limitations:**
- Effects are tiny in absolute terms.
- Rider-level randomization on a shared driver pool. That quoted pickup windows for incumbents were identical across arms is asserted, but this check did not verify it.
- All-session estimates condition on returning riders, so treatment could change the sample.
- COVID-era period.
- The authors no longer have the micro-data needed to rescale by first-session availability.
- The salience mechanism is consistent with the data, but it is not uniquely identified.
**Why selected:** The cleanest field test this week of a core axiom of demand theory, with direct implications for product-line design and welfare.

### 2. Andrabi, Bau, Das, Khwaja, Michaud-Leclerc — Learn to Earn: The Long-Term Effects of Foundational Learning (NBER WP 36030, 16 Nov 2026, v1; AEARCTR-0000242) https://www.nber.org/papers/w36030
**Question:** Do primary-school learning gains in a low-income country carry through to adult schooling and earnings?
**Method:** LEAPS panel: 5,865 children in 112 Punjab villages followed 2003–2018. Attrition is 15.5% (≈1%/yr), with no differential attrition reported.
- Design (i): exposure to school value-added (SVA) through school switches, conditional on first-school fixed effects. Switches are mostly sibling-logistics moves. Robustness uses a nearby-school IV and a movers design.
- Design (ii): the 2004 village-level report-card RCT, which raised scores 0.11 sd.
**Result:**
- 1 sd higher SVA gives +0.16–0.19 sd adult cognitive skills, +1 year of schooling, +10 pp high-school diploma, and +22 log points earnings among earners (+25 for men).
- RCT: +0.09 sd test scores in 2018 and +0.54 years of schooling. Older cohort (aged 9–11 in 2003): +$10.7/month (SE 5.2; randomization-inference p = 0.040) and +11.8 log points among earners (RI p = 0.049).
- MVPF = ∞ for men; benefit-cost ratio 2.6–3.4; IRR 11.8–13.1%.
**Beyond prior work:** The first causal learning→earnings link in a low-income country. It uses two independent designs whose earnings/learning ratios agree, and it separates learning from years of schooling: the gains are 5–9× the Mincerian return.
**Surviving claim:** Primary-school learning gains in rural Pakistan raised schooling and, for men who have finished school, earnings 15 years later. The magnitudes are consistent across observational and experimental variation.
**Decisive limitations:**
- RCT earnings effects appear only in the older-cohort subgroup. They are marginal (RI p ≈ 0.04–0.05, ~108 clusters) and come from a family of outcomes and subgroups. The MVPF = ∞ headline rests on them.
- Earnings are measured at ages 20–30, and 13% of the sample is still enrolled.
- Female labor-force participation (7%) leaves women's returns essentially unidentified.
**Why selected:** It answers a first-order development-policy question with unusually long, low-attrition data and a convergent experimental check.

### 3. Gagliardone, Gertler, Lenzu, Tielens, Ebsim — An Odd Approximation of Inflation for State Dependent Pricing Models (NBER WP 36016, 16 Nov 2026, v1) https://www.nber.org/papers/w36016
**Question:** Can state-dependent pricing models (random menu costs plus strategic complementarities) be brought into standard DSGE solution and estimation? And what changes when they are?
**Method:** A third-order aggregation result: inflation ≈ h(0) + 3φσ²x + φx³ + ψ, in the mean, variance and skewness of price gaps. Along an impulse response this collapses to a cubic ("odd") polynomial in the average gap. The model is embedded in a textbook NK model and solved by third-order perturbation, with checks against the global solution for 1/3/9% shocks. It is then estimated on US and euro-area data (2000Q1–2025Q1) with a particle smoother, using inflation, GDP growth, the policy rate and the frequency of price adjustment.
**Result:**
- During the 2021–22 surge, the cost-push share of surge inflation falls from 48.2% (linear/Calvo Kalman smoother) to 6.6% in the US, and from 60.9% to 22.0% in the euro area. Demand absorbs most of the difference.
- Frequency and selection account for ≈½ (US) and ≈⅖ (EA) of peak model-implied inflation.
- The effective Phillips-curve slope rises ~¾ (US 2021–22) and more than doubles (EA 2022).
- Looking through the surge costs 2–3× the Calvo prediction.
**Beyond prior work:** A closed-form state-space representation that keeps selection. Blanco et al. (2026) abstract from selection; Auclert et al. (2024/2026) are first-order or numerical sequence-space methods. This one gives filtering and historical decompositions with standard tools.
**Surviving claim:** A tractable, estimable menu-cost block. In a one-sector NK model it reassigns most "cost-push" inflation in large-shock episodes to endogenous frequency/selection amplification of demand.
**Decisive limitations:** The cost-push compression is relative to a three-shock, one-sector model. The surge's sectoral and relative-price supply shocks (energy, supply chains) have no separate channel, so the "demand" share could be overstated. Third-order accuracy and the interaction/residual terms matter for the largest shocks.
**Why selected:** A methodological advance likely to be widely adopted, with a consequential, testable reinterpretation of the 2021–22 inflation.

### 4. Hollingsworth, Ortega, Zirogiannis — Decoding Success: Phonics-based Instruction and Student Achievement (NBER WP 36018, 16 Nov 2026, v1) https://www.nber.org/papers/w36018
**Question:** Did Reading First, NCLB's ≈$1bn/yr phonics program declared ineffective in 2008, raise achievement and adult outcomes?
**Method:** Texas administrative data (Texas ERC), K-12 through earnings. DiD and event study (1995–2017, reference year 2003) of district eligibility, which was set by the 2002 third-grade reading fail rate (cutoff 13.6%) and poverty. Robustness uses a ±5 pp bandwidth around the cutoff. A simulated-instrument IV uses the funding formula (first-stage F ≈ 1,711). Item-level analysis of digitized exams.
**Result:**
- Third-grade reading +~0.1 sd. The effect grows to ~0.15 sd by 2012 and persists through grade 8.
- Gains are concentrated on decoding items and on word-heavy math items.
- Higher on-time high-school graduation, college enrollment and four-year completion.
- Earnings at age 25 +$509 (≈1.5%).
- Eligible districts spent $131/K-3 student more from Reading First funds.
**Beyond prior work:** Reverses the policy-defining federal null (Gamse et al. 2008), attributed to that study's short exposure window. It is the first long-run evidence on a scaled phonics mandate, now relevant as 40+ states legislate the "science of reading".
**Surviving claim:** In Texas, phonics-tied Reading First money produced lasting reading gains and modest long-run attainment and earnings gains.
**Decisive limitations:**
- Assignment is by 2002 fail rates and poverty.
- The test regime switched (TAAS→TAKS) exactly at the 2003 reference year and again (→STAAR) in 2012.
- The raw gap jumps ~0.11 sd in 2004, faster than the gradual event-study path.
- The Texas Student Success Initiative (third-grade retention) coincides with the program.
- Single state, and the Gamse et al. design is not replicated.
**Why selected:** High policy stakes, long administrative follow-up, and a credible pre-period. Figure 2A shows a flat raw gap 1995–2003, which mitigates mean reversion around the 2002 selection year.

### w36013 Anti-compromise — traditions: revealed-preference/stochastic choice (Samuelson; Luce; McFadden; Manzini–Mariotti), rational inattention (Sims; Matějka–McKay; Caplin–Dean), field/platform experimentation (Harrison–List taxonomy; Johari et al. on marketplace interference)
**Summary:** A randomized premium ride tier at Lyft increased demand for the cheapest option and for not buying. The effect rises with the premium price and with exposure. Rational inference and inattention fit poorly; a memory-cued price-salience model fits.
- **Warrant / interference (Experimental; MI5, GE5):** 🟡 critical-for-mechanism / hidden / unresolved.
  - Treated and control riders share drivers. Fast Pass availability depends on a nearby driver, and priority dispatch could shift incumbents' quoted or realized wait times.
  - Balance in quoted windows is not shown in the main text.
  - Defense: prices are identical, and the 1–2.5% treated take-up limits supply effects.
  - Repair: local (show quoted ETAs by arm).
- **Warrant / selection (Causal):** 🟡 fixable / tricky / suspicion. The all-session panel conditions on continued app use, which treatment itself shifts (not buying ↑). The 4–5× growth may partly be composition. Repair: local (fixed-horizon, rider-level outcomes).
- **Calibration (Integrity: reporting consistency):** 🟡 fixable / obvious / verified.
  - The introduction states the not-buying effect equals a 1.17% uniform price rise; Section 2.2 says 0.89%.
  - Treatment-on-treated scalings divide by an average 33% availability because first-session micro-data are no longer available.
- **Alternatives:** 🟢 / tricky / unresolved. The salience/memory model is sufficient but not uniquely identified. Reference-price dependence is a near-equivalent account.
- **Precedence:** 🟢. A novel field direction relative to Gu et al. (2018), Rafai et al. (2022) and Cai (2026).

### w36030 Learn to Earn — traditions: human capital/returns (Mincer; Card 1999; Heckman–Lochner–Todd), value-added validation (Kane–Staiger; Chetty–Friedman–Rockoff), development-RCT inference (Young 2019; Deaton–Cartwright)
**Summary:** Two designs (SVA exposure through switches, and a village RCT) give matching learning→schooling→earnings effects 15 years on. Benefit-cost ratios are high.
- **Inference (ME8; multiple testing):** 🟡 critical-for-headline / tricky / verified (Table 8). RCT earnings effects come only in the older-cohort subgroup (RI p = 0.040 for levels, 0.049 for logs). The full-sample men's effect is not significant, there are many outcomes, and MVPF = ∞ rests on this. Defense: the subgroup is motivated by schooling completion and matches the SVA estimates. Repair: local (family-wise adjustment; check whether the long-term analysis was pre-specified).
- **Warrant (Causal):** 🟡 fixable / tricky / suspicion. SVA identification assumes school switches are conditionally exogenous given the first school. Defense: balance on level and trend; sibling-driven switching; IV and movers designs agree; the RCT corroborates.
- **Calibration (GE7):** 🟡 fixable / obvious / verified.
  - Lifetime extrapolation from ages 24–26.
  - $175k "per school" = $1,650 × 106 students.
  - Women's returns are essentially unidentified because of low labor-force participation.
- **Precedence:** 🟢. Novel for a low-income country; prior long-run evidence is mostly from schooling access.

### w36016 Odd approximation — traditions: state-dependent pricing (Caballero–Engel; Golosov–Lucas; Alvarez–Lippi), heterogeneous-agent computation (Auclert–Rognlie–Straub sequence-space), Bayesian DSGE shock identification (Smets–Wouters; Fernández-Villaverde–Rubio-Ramírez; An–Schorfheide)
**Summary:** The paper derives a three-moment state-space representation of menu-cost inflation dynamics that can be estimated with perturbation. Applied to 2021–22, it shrinks the cost-push share (US 48%→7%) because frequency and selection amplify demand.
- **Alternatives (MA; ME3):** 🟠 critical-for-headline / tricky / unresolved. The compression is relative to a one-sector, three-shock model. Sectoral/energy relative-price shocks have no channel, so the "demand" share may absorb supply-side dispersion. Defense: frequency is an observable and the model matches its timing. Repair: substantial (multi-sector or relative-price shocks).
- **Numerical correctness (Computational):** 🟡 fixable / hidden / unresolved.
  - Third-order accuracy for the largest shocks; the global-solution comparison covers only 1/3/9% cost-push impulses.
  - The "joint interaction" and "data minus joint" residuals take a non-trivial share.
  - Particle-smoother seed variation is reported.
- **Precedence:** 🟢. Distinct from Blanco et al. (2026), which has no selection, and from Auclert et al. (2024/2026), which are first-order or numerical.
