### 1. Finkelstein, Notowidigdo, Rafkin — When Food Stamps Became Free (NBER WP 35996, 9 Nov 2026, v1) https://www.nber.org/papers/w35996
Question: What did the 1979 removal of the food-stamp purchase requirement (subsidy 65%→100%) do to enrollment, infant health and recipients' valuation?
Method: County exposure design (1970 poverty share minus 1972 enrollment rate), event studies 1972–81, IV scaling; rollout replication (Almond et al. 2011); revealed-preference valuation from the enrollment response; welfare under redistributive weights.
Result: Enrollment +1.85 pp (SE 0.15), ~25%, new enrollees poorer. No detectable birth-weight gain; 95% CI excludes gains >2% per enrollee. Recipients act as if free stamps worth 59¢/$ (nonparametric bounds 42–77¢). Still welfare-improving under standard weights because of targeting; free stamps improved targeting on social value, worsened it on private value.
Beyond prior work: First credible estimate of the copay→free margin for SNAP; turns an old design question (Nichols–Zeckhauser ordeals) into measured private vs social targeting.
Surviving claim: Making in-kind food aid free drew in substantially poorer households who value it well below cash, with no infant-health dividend, yet raised social welfare under conventional weights.
Decisive limitations: Exposure variation is mostly 1970 poverty (authors' statement) → exposed to other 1979–82 shocks; valuation rests on an enrollment-cost model with wide bounds; 1970s program design limits transport to EBT-era SNAP.
Why selected: Cleanest identification/welfare mapping of the week on a $100bn/yr program; directly relevant to current premium/copay debates.

### 2. Boehm, Kim, Kroner — The Main Monetary Policy Shock (NBER WP 36004, 9 Nov 2026, v1; also CESifo WP 13117) https://www.nber.org/papers/w36004
Question: Are U.S. monetary policy shocks really unimportant for post-1990 business cycles once multidimensional FOMC news is accounted for?
Method: VAR (1996m1–2025m7) with three high-frequency surprises (near-term rate, 2–30y path, Boehm–Kroner non-yield shock) as variables; MMP shock = linear combination maximising 4-year FEV share of IP and headline CPI; block bootstrap.
Result: MMP explains ~9% of IP, ~18% of CPI, ~16% of the broad dollar (4-yr average FEV); authors call these lower bounds. A contractionary MMP shock barely moves rates, persistently lowers headline CPI (mostly via oil; core only mildly), appreciates the dollar, lowers net exports, temporarily tightens financial conditions and IP.
Beyond prior work: Overturns the Ramey (2016)/Plagborg-Møller–Wolf (2022) "shocks don't matter" reading; discrepancy traced to the non-yield shock (vs. Bauer–Schmeling–Schrimpf 2026).
Surviving claim: FOMC-window news not spanned by the yield curve accounts for a non-trivial share of U.S. CPI and dollar fluctuations.
Decisive limitations: Whether the non-yield component is monetary policy or central-bank information/risk news is not resolved (unchanged rates + oil-led CPI decline is also an information-shock signature); max-share selection can inflate in-sample FEV shares — unclear whether bootstrap re-optimises.
Why selected: Highest-stakes macro claim of the week, transparently identified, testable.

### 3. Hanna, Breza, Olken, Banerjee, Chandrasekhar, Moore — Target What? The Foundations of Poverty Targeting (NBER WP 35984, 9 Nov 2026, v1) https://www.nber.org/papers/w35984
Question: Do "lowest consumption", "lowest self-assessed welfare" and "largest marginal social valuation (MSV)" pick the same households?
Method: 600 Indonesian villages (2010–11 PKH setting), >5,500 respondents each rank neighbours on level and on how much a transfer would help; compared with consumption and Cantril-type welfare; RCT of PMT vs self-targeting vs community targeting.
Result: Consumption targeting misses 77% of top-MSV households; self-assessed welfare misses 57% (347/612); community level ranks miss 25%. Raw consumption–welfare correlation 0.33. More consensus on MSV than chance. PMT tracks consumption, ordeals track self-assessed levels, community targeting is closest to MSV.
Beyond prior work: First direct field measure of community MSV, separating levels from margins; reframes targeting-method choice as a normative choice.
Surviving claim: Standard targeting metrics diverge materially from the community's view of who gains most, and method choice implicitly selects the welfare criterion.
Decisive limitations: "Miss" rates are not reliability-adjusted for consumption/self-report noise (noise test covers only MSV vs level ranks); community MSV may encode deservingness/social ties rather than welfare gains; 15-year-old data reused from earlier experiments.
Why selected: Conceptually consequential for every PMT-based transfer program; normative point made empirically.

### 4. Chernov, Kelly, Malamud, Schwab — The Hidden Sharpe Ratio (NBER WP 35988, 9 Nov 2026, v1) https://www.nber.org/papers/w35988
Question: How large is the maximal Sharpe ratio in large cross-sections when P/T is not small?
Method: Ridge-regularised, debiased GRS-type estimator with two-sided bounds and asymptotic CIs (proportional regime P/T→c); 132 equity characteristics (JKP 2023), 65 bond characteristics, random-feature portfolios.
Result: Max annualised Sharpe ≈7.4 for U.S. equities (5.0 ex-micro-caps; rolling 7–11), vs 1.0–4.0 portfolio benchmarks; ridge-Markowitz portfolios fall short by a non-vanishing factor ≈1.8 in squared SR (ceiling ≈5.5 / 3.7); bonds add insignificantly; nonlinear > linear; post-2004 characteristics add little.
Beyond prior work: Two-sided bounds and a shortfall theorem for ridge portfolios, extending Chernov et al. (2025).
Surviving claim: Within the published-characteristic universe, the in-sample frictionless maximum Sharpe ratio is far above what implementable regularised portfolios capture.
Decisive limitations: Gross of trading costs, full-sample, constant-moment population quantity; characteristic universe selected ex post by published predictability (estimator corrects sampling noise, not selection); implied SDF volatility >7/yr strains any equilibrium reading.
Why selected: Striking, methodologically careful number that will be cited; readers need the scope caveats attached.

### w35996 Food stamps — traditions: optimal-tax/in-kind (Atkinson–Stiglitz; Nichols–Zeckhauser 1982), design-based causal (Imbens–Rubin; Goldsmith-Pinkham–Sorkin–Swift 2020), fetal-origins (Almond–Currie 2011)
Summary: 1979 elimination of the purchase requirement raised enrollment ~25% toward poorer households without detectable birth-weight gains. Recipients value free stamps ≈59¢/$, yet targeting makes the program welfare-improving.
- Warrant/alternatives (Causal; GE5): 🟡 fixable / tricky / suspicion — exposure "operates primarily through the poverty rate" (Sec. 2.2) so poorer counties' 1979–82 shocks could load on it. Defense: low-SNAP-industry cycle controls + Freyaldenhoven proxy purge slight pre-trends; magnitudes stable. Impact: enrollment level modestly; health null robust. Repair: local.
- Calibration (LP7, GE7): 🟡 fixable / obvious / unresolved — 59¢ needs an enrollment-cost model; bounds 42–77¢. Defense: welfare sign reported robust. Repair: local.
- Scope (DE1-type comparability): 🟢 fixable / obvious — 1970s coupons vs EBT-era SNAP.
### w36004 MMP shock — traditions: SVAR identification (Sims–Zha; Uhlig max-share; Plagborg-Møller–Wolf), information-effect school (Romer–Romer 2000; Nakamura–Steinsson 2018; Jarociński–Karadi 2020), narrative macro (Ramey 2016)
Summary: Max-share combination of three HF surprises explains ~9% IP, ~18% CPI, ~16% dollar FEV. Contractionary shock leaves rates flat, cuts headline CPI via oil, appreciates the dollar.
- Identification (MA10, ME3): 🟠 critical / tricky / unresolved — assumes surprises spanned exclusively by MP shocks; non-yield shock may carry information/risk news; flat rates + oil-led CPI fall fits an information shock. Defense: no structural rotation claimed; expectations fall. Impact: headline weakens to "FOMC-window news matters". Repair: substantial (sign/high-frequency co-movement restrictions on NY component).
- Inference (ME5, ME8): 🟡 fixable / hidden / unresolved — max-share selection inflates in-sample FEV; block bootstrap reported, re-optimisation per draw not verified. Repair: local (placebo combinations, re-optimised bootstrap).
- Precedence: 🟢 — closest Bauer–Schmeling–Schrimpf (2026); novelty = non-yield shock at business-cycle frequency.
### w35984 Target What? — traditions: social choice/capabilities (Arrow; Sen; Saez–Stantcheva weights), household-survey measurement (Deaton 1997; Groves et al.), ordeals/self-selection (Nichols–Zeckhauser; Besley–Coate 1992)

- Measurement (Survey; DE4, LP10): 🟡 critical-for-headline / tricky / suspicion — 77%/57% misses not adjusted for reliability of single-round consumption and 5-point self-report; formal noise test covers MSV vs level only. - Normative bridge: 🟢 fixable / obvious / unresolved — community MSV may encode deservingness/kinship; authors frame targeting as normative choice.
- Provenance: 🟢 / hidden / unresolved — 2010–11 data, RCT arms from earlier experiments; timing/pre-specification of MSV module not checked.
