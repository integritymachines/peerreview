4. ORIGINAL REVIEW CALIBRATION TO PRESERVE

The original workflow runs ten independent reviews and then a final deciding
run using the union of flags. It is not a one-pass review with optional repeats.

Roles:
S: system/coordinator and scorecard keeper.
A: assigning editor.
R1-RN: three to eight reviewers.
H: reviewer huddle.
E: deciding editor, the same person as A.

Original sequence and information flow:

- S pre-scans, identifies focus/competencies, checks integrity, chooses A and
  provides A a brief qualitative preliminary assessment. Preserve the existing
  integrity marker and preliminary assessment unless separately changed.
- A performs breadth review, selects the panel from the relevant competencies
  and flaw pointers, and assigns each reviewer a textbook and specialist paper.
- A's flag/hunch report goes privately to E, not to the discovery reviewers
  or initial H.
- Reviewers conduct flag-blind breadth review. Each self-assigns an additional
  textbook and specialist paper.
- H conducts depth review: what fails, exactly where, and with what consequence.
  The original goal is consensus; the text also records splits/unresolved issues.
- E compares A's report against the reviewer/H findings and reconvenes H where
  findings are missed or material questions/disagreements remain.
- E's callback can disclose A's discrepancies/report to H at that stage.
- E finalizes the scorecard; S maintains it.
- S retains the run history. The final deciding run receives the union of flags,
  all prior reviewer identities/assignments and assigned/self-assigned literature.
- The original asks for minimal progress reports during runs, a full record of
  the deciding run, and a compact combined scorecard.

Other calibration to preserve unless the user accepts an exact change:

- Highest scholarly/journal standard and novelty expectation.
- A=E; the specified panel size; all previous reviewers in the deciding run.
- Flaw-driven competency selection and reviewers' blindness to A's flags.
- The prescribed book and paper assignments, without invented exceptions.
- Fatal/critical/fixable severity; obvious/tricky/concealed visibility.
- Existing colors, integrity markers, and huddle reconventions.
- Novelty/significance cannot override fatal methodological/integrity flaws.
- Pinpoint location, quotation, citation, consequence and terse justification.
- The final human-facing scorecard's instruction not to use flag codes.
- Housekeeping advice remains distinct from issues affecting rigor.
- Merit-based review without author/journal reputation deciding the assessment.

Do not infer permission to redefine these just because part of the prompt is
being modernized. The baseline has some internal overlaps/wording tensions;
they require narrow ONJ proposals if they are to be changed.

6.2 Proposal 2: rejected -- generic traditions were the wrong interpretation

The assistant proposed replacing named professors with generic traditions and
removing their particular scholarly/rank anchors. The user rejected that.

The user's subsequent explanation is controlling:

"The reason we use named scholars: to guarantee perspective variability and to
anchor the review in a particular level, something that doesn't happen if we
create fake personae. The problem is that we cannot actually claim the real
existing persons approved of the review, hence the bridge of using the
'research traditions' of named scholars. Two consequences: we anchor the
reviewer deliberately in the published output of that scholar, and we don't
make ourselves vulnerable to a claim we used digital twins of actual people.
In practice, this simply replaces 'Heckman' with 'Heckman tradition'."

Settled intent:
- Keep named scholars as deliberate perspective and scholarly-level anchors.
- Ground the review perspective in the named scholar's published output.
- Use labels such as "Heckman tradition".
- Do not claim actual personal participation, approval or endorsement.
- Do not substitute invented generic experts or anonymous traditions.
- Do not use this change to remove the original competence/rank requirements.

The assistant's latest proposed bridge sentence, not yet accepted:
